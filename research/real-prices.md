# Real prices of the Framework upgrades

(Researched by Claude)

What each Cidery node would cost to buy from Framework. Coin costs are from [device-trees.md](device-trees.md#framework).

Source: frame.work US store (configurators + Marketplace), checked 9 Oct 2026. USD, before tax. Several prices are sales (marked *sale*) and RAM prices have been moving a lot in 2026, so treat these as a snapshot.

**How to read it**
- *Part price* = buying that upgrade on its own from the Marketplace.
- *Coins per $* = node cost ÷ part price. **Lower is a better deal** (you get more real money per coin).
- The Framework 12 has **one** RAM slot and **one** SSD slot, so every RAM/SSD upgrade replaces the old part. The part price is the whole new module, not the difference.

- **Most of the tree is priced at about 8 coins per $ of difference, so 1 coin ≈ $0.12.** The RAM and SSD steps are almost exactly 8⅓ (25 coins per $3).
- Asked whether the prices were specced at $1 = 8 coins, miggy said "perchance (about that)" (10 Oct). So 8 coins per $ looks like the design, and the off-pattern nodes are probably rounding or older prices.
- Off-pattern nodes: Ethernet as a Fancy card (3.4) and the Framework 13 (7.1) are good deals. The $19 Fancy cards (11.1), USB card (10.0) and 2.8K display (10.0) are bad ones.
- The 2.8K number uses the replacement-kit difference because the configurator option is out of stock. With its +$130 launch price it would be 6.9.
- Comparing standalone board prices instead of configurator prices gives Core 3 +$30, i5 +$70, Core 5 +$80, Ryzen +$200. That's noisier (13.3, 5.7, 18.8, 11.3) because the 13th Gen boards are on sale.
- The Max rows add up every step's difference (the DIY Edition costs coins but has no real difference). They're less than "Max − base" in the next section because the $699 base price includes Windows.

### Best and worst value (part prices)

This uses the *Per node* table (coins ÷ the part's full price). By real difference (the fairer view), almost everything is ≈ 8 coins per $, with Ethernet as a Fancy card (3.4) and the Framework 13 (7.1) the only good deals, and the $19 Fancy cards (11.1), USB card (10.0) and 2.8K display (10.0) the bad ones.


- **Best:** Framework 13 (0.6), i5-1334U (1.1), Core 3 304 (1.3). These are cheap in coins and expensive in real life, and they're all forced steps on the way to the 13 anyway.
- **Worst:** USB card (10.0), Stylus (8.5), and the $19 Fancy cards (5.3). Each card is 100 coins for a $10–19 part. If you buy Fancy cards, Ethernet ($39) gets you the most for your coins.
- RAM, SSD, Core 5 320, 2.8K and Ryzen all land at roughly 3–4 coins per $.

## Max builds in real money

What the finished laptop would cost new, configured directly (not the sum of every step):

| Build | Coins | Real equivalent | USD |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Base laptop | 0 | Laptop 12 pre-built, i3-1315U, 8GB, 512GB | $699 |
| **Max 12** | 10700 | Laptop 12 DIY Core 5 320 ($779) + 48GB ($659) + 2TB ($495) + Stylus ($59) + 4 Fancy at $19 + 1 USB ($86) | **≈ $2,078** |
| **Max 13** | 13850 | Laptop 13 DIY Ryzen AI 7 350 ($1,129) + 48GB ($659) + 2TB SN7100 ($505) + 4 Fancy at $19 + 1 USB ($86) | **≈ $2,379** + 2.8K display |

- Max 13 doesn't include the 2.8K panel because it's out of stock as an option. Add up to $249 (the price of the kit). At launch the option was +$130 (Tom's Hardware review).
- Using Ethernet for all 4 Fancy cards instead of $19 cards adds $80.
- None of these include an OS or power adapter. The DIY editions sell them separately.
- Buying every step separately like the game does (Core 3 → i5 → Core 5 boards, then 16 → 32 → 48GB sticks, and so on) would cost far more: about $3,330 for Max 12 and $4,960 for Max 13. Nobody would actually do that, so the end-state prices are the fairer comparison.
